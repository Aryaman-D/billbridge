Bill drafting 101

Big change starts
with clear words.

A bill is a proposal to create, change, or repeal a law. The exact format and process vary by state, but a strong draft starts by answering a few simple questions.

Try this: “This proposal would address [problem] by requiring [who] to [action].”
01   Give it a clear title

Summarize the subject of your proposal in a short, specific title so readers understand its purpose.

02   Explain the problem and purpose

Describe the issue, who it affects, and what your proposal aims to improve. Evidence and real examples can help explain why it matters.

03   Define the key terms

Clarify important words, the people or organizations covered, and any exceptions. Specific language helps avoid confusion.

04   Set out what would change

Explain the required or prohibited actions, who is responsible, how the policy would be implemented, and any funding or enforcement needed.

05   Include timing and review

Consider when the law would take effect and whether its results should be reviewed. Check your state’s drafting rules and existing laws.

A place to begin.

Use the template to organize your thoughts, then see how its sections might look when filled in. This is a starting point, not an official state form; drafting rules and required language differ by state.

Your starting point

Basic bill template

Replace the prompts with details about your idea. You can copy this into your own document.

BILL TITLE: [A short, descriptive title]

PURPOSE: [What problem does this proposal address, and what is it intended to accomplish?]

SECTION 1. DEFINITIONS
[Define important terms and identify who or what is covered.]

SECTION 2. PROPOSED POLICY
[State the action required or prohibited, who is responsible, and any exceptions.]

SECTION 3. IMPLEMENTATION
[Explain which agency or entity would carry out the policy and how. Address funding, enforcement, or reporting if needed.]

SECTION 4. EFFECTIVE DATE
[State when the proposal would take effect.]
Illustrative example

School water access

A fictional example showing how the same structure can describe a proposed policy. It is not an enacted bill.

BILL TITLE: School Water Refill Access Act

PURPOSE: To make drinking water easier to access in public schools and reduce reliance on single-use plastic bottles.

SECTION 1. DEFINITIONS
“Public school” means a school operated by a public school district. “Water refill station” means a fixture that provides potable water for filling reusable bottles.

SECTION 2. PROPOSED POLICY
Each public school district shall assess access to water refill stations at its schools and develop a plan to address identified gaps.

SECTION 3. IMPLEMENTATION
The state education agency shall provide guidance for district assessments. Districts shall submit their plans to the agency within one year. Funding and implementation details would need further development.

SECTION 4. EFFECTIVE DATE
This proposal would take effect on a date established by the legislature.

Before pursuing a proposal, check your state legislature’s drafting requirements, relevant existing law, costs, and implementation details. Legislative staff or qualified legal counsel can help refine the final language.
